About the author

From pain to purpose.

My name is Muhammad Sow. I’m 24, and I’m still figuring life out.

I’ve always been someone who thinks a lot, sometimes probably more than I should. I notice the little things: conversations, changes in people, quiet moments, and feelings that are easy to overlook. I think that’s one of the reasons I ended up writing.

I write about love, heartbreak, loneliness, family, growing up, and the complicated parts of being human. I’m interested in the moments that don’t always look important when they happen, but somehow stay with us for years.

I don’t really believe people are meant to have everything figured out. I think we’re all learning as we go. We make mistakes, lose people, fall in love, get hurt, change, and sometimes have to start over.

That’s what I try to put into my writing.

My first book, A Thousand Miles of Tears: My Journey From Ashes to Light, is a deeply personal account of hardship, resilience, transformation, and hope. Through it, I explore the roads I never expected to travel, the wounds I’ve carried, and the strength I’ve discovered by choosing to keep walking.

I don’t want my characters to feel perfect. I want them to feel like people. People who say the wrong thing sometimes. People who are scared but still stay. People who love each other but don’t always know how to show it. People who carry things quietly and keep going anyway.

The Parts We Don’t Fix is probably the best example of that. It’s a story about love, but underneath that, it’s about life itself. It’s about the things we can change, the things we can’t, the people who shape us, and learning that not everything broken needs to be repaired.

Sometimes, we just have to learn how to live with it.

And maybe that’s something I’m still learning too.

Writing has become one of the ways I make sense of all those things. It allows me to turn experiences, questions, pain, hope, and the things I don’t always know how to say into something that might reach someone else.

This is the story I’m still writing.

Muhammad Sow
